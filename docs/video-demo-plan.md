# DCF Inspiring Personalities: Video Demo Plan

Length: about 2 minutes 45 seconds. Audience: first-time visitors and new DCF users. Goal: show what the site is, why it can be trusted, and the four things to try (browse, read a profile, check a source, ask a question), with a short look at Resources for editors.

The example person throughout is Rabindranath Tagore, because his profile has several lenses (including educator) and plenty of sources. Narration is about 390 words, which is a relaxed pace for this length.

## Scene 1. Opening (0:00 to 0:15)

On screen: the home page with the particle intro animation playing, then settling on the heading.

Narration: "This is DCF Inspiring Personalities, a collection of 25 remarkable people from Indian civilization, from ancient sages to modern scientists. What makes it different is simple: every statement you read is tied to a real source you can check."

## Scene 2. Explore (0:15 to 0:40)

On screen: click Explore. Show the five groups. Use the role filter, then the era filter, then clear them. Type "Tagore" in the search box. Scroll briefly through the timeline.

Narration: "Explore shows everyone, arranged in five groups: sages, spiritual teachers and reformers, scientists and builders, rulers and freedom fighters, and artists and nation builders. You can filter by role, era and region, search by name, or follow the timeline to see who lived when. Let's open Rabindranath Tagore."

## Scene 3. A profile (0:40 to 1:10)

On screen: open the Tagore profile. Scroll slowly through the introduction and two or three sections. Under "View Tagore as", choose Educator and show the statements reorder. Point the cursor at the page address to show the lens is in the link.

Narration: "Each profile is built from small, separate statements, organised into sections like context, contributions, challenges and relevance today. The 'View as' options let you see a person through one lens. Here, as an educator, the most relevant statements come forward. The facts don't change, only the emphasis. You can share this exact view by copying the link."

## Scene 4. Checking a source (1:10 to 1:30)

On screen: click a citation number. Hold on the source panel showing title, author, publisher and the supporting passage. Close it. Scroll to a limited-information note if one is visible, then to Related lives.

Narration: "Click any citation number to see where a statement comes from: the book or record, who published it, and the exact passage behind it. Where good evidence doesn't exist yet, the profile says so honestly instead of filling the gap. At the end, Related lives suggests who to read next."

## Scene 5. Ask (1:30 to 2:15)

On screen: open Ask. Tagore is already chosen (or choose him in the person picker). Pick Balanced depth. Click the suggested question "What did Rabindranath Tagore do that mattered?" Let the answer appear. Click one citation in the answer. Then click the follow-up "In its time". Finally, hover over the star rating.

Narration: "Ask lets you put your own questions. Choose a person, pick how deep you want the answer, from Simple to Research, and ask, or use one of the suggestions. Ask answers from DCF's approved library first, and only searches credible sources on the web when it needs to. Every answer cites its sources, so you can check them in the same way. Follow-ups like 'In its time' help you keep exploring. And if an answer could be better, rate it. DCF's editors read every rating."

## Scene 6. Resources, for DCF editors (2:15 to 2:35)

On screen: the Resources page already signed in (never show the admin key being typed). Click across the five sections: People, Sources and claims, Feedback, Knowledge engine. Open one claim to show its supporting passage and the Approve button.

Narration: "Behind the scenes, DCF editors use Resources to add people, check sources and approve each statement before it appears. AI helps find and suggest material, but people decide what is published. Editors can also review reader feedback and control what automatic research may do and spend."

## Scene 7. Close (2:35 to 2:45)

On screen: return to the Explore page, then fade to the site address.

Narration: "Start with someone you admire, follow the sources, and ask your own questions. DCF Inspiring Personalities: learning from great lives, grounded in evidence."

## Recording checklist

Record at 1920 by 1080 in a clean browser window: no bookmarks bar, no other tabs, notifications off, browser zoom around 110 to 125 percent so text is readable on phones. Use light mode for consistency.

Run the Ask question once before recording. Answers are cached for 24 hours, so the second time it appears almost instantly and the video does not show a long wait.

Sign in to Resources before recording and never show the admin key on screen. Avoid showing costs, the Knowledge engine budget figures or diagnostics, which are internal.

Move the mouse slowly and pause for about a second after each click so viewers can follow. A cursor highlight helps.

Record the narration separately in a quiet room and lay it over the screen recording, then cut the screen recording to match. Add captions, because many people watch without sound.

Optionally add short on-screen labels at each scene: Explore, Profiles and lenses, Check the source, Ask, For editors.

## What to leave out

The technical details (how the database, search and AI services work), the Extra diagnostics section, web search rules, and anything about costs or limits. These are covered in the written guide. The video should only show what a new user will see and do.
