# M. S. Subbulakshmi corpus — 12 October 2026

Added only M. S. Subbulakshmi through the generic category, field, source, chunk, claim, and epistemic-review tables. The reusable **Musicians, Performers and Cultural Figures** framework has twelve fields. Generic AI lenses offered for the category are Artistic / Musical, Cultural, Social, Gender, Institutional, Spiritual, and International influence.

**13 linked sources; 57 reviewable draft claims; 56 active source passages.** All source anchors passed the ingestion dry run. Sources have `ai_candidate` provenance and approved-source availability for AI retrieval; claims remain draft with proposed epistemic types and require human review. No human approval was simulated.

The sources are the Music Academy’s 1968 souvenir, award list and journal; the Sangeet Natak Akademi fellowship citation; the 1966 UN press statement; the 1970 *Yojana* concert account; the Ramon Magsaysay Foundation citation and Subbulakshmi’s response; the 1998 Bharat Ratna Gazette notice; the Press Information Bureau centenary profile; Prasar Bharati’s National Artists list; Saregama’s catalogue; Keshav Desiraju’s biographer interview; and Garrett Field’s UCLA ethnomusicology article. The UN PDF and Mercatus interview were readable through web archives/search but intermittently refused direct runtime downloads; the runner records short checked transcript excerpts for those two and flags the access limit in Resources.

Coverage: musical household and training; Carnatic concert culture; early academy career, 1944 Bombay conference, *Meera*, recordings and major composers; raga style and critical assessments; devotional repertoire and reception; Edinburgh, BBC, Cairo and UN appearances; Sadasivam, Gandhi, Sarojini Naidu and Kanchi relationships; gendered access, Pattammal and other contemporaries; benefit concerts; major awards, including her 1968 first woman Sangita Kalanidhi and 1998 Bharat Ratna. The corpus explains audience expansion and later influence without claiming sole causation.

Qualifications: the UN press statement heads the concert **21 October 1966**, while a later UN archival caption, the 1968 Music Academy account and the 1970 *Yojana* account use **23 October**. The corpus says October 1966 and preserves the discrepancy. Menon’s musical judgments, Desiraju’s criticism and recollections, institutional praise, and Subbulakshmi’s own devotional statement have distinct epistemic labels. The Gandhi recording story and Kanchi dialogue are treated as reported recollection. Her devotional fame should not erase the documented classical programme and rigor. The record does not establish that she alone opened the stage to women; Pattammal and other women were significant contemporaries.

Remaining gaps: comprehensive discography with original issue dates; direct programme or audio study across decades; deeper archival work on hereditary-musician caste and gender history; rigorous tracing of influence on named later artists.

Each of the six requested representative questions returned HTTP 200 with a complete cited answer after two initial abstentions exposed gaps in influence and gender context. The corpus added attributed biographer and ethnomusicology evidence to address those gaps. Typecheck, changed-file ESLint and focused context/lens tests passed (19 tests).
