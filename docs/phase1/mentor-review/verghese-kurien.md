## Important qualities

- Operation Flood began in 1970 and used village milk-producer cooperatives, urban markets and dairy services to expand milk production and marketing. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Phase III, 1985–96, strengthened procurement and marketing infrastructure and extended veterinary, feed and insemination services rather than simply creating new outlets. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- NDDB describes village producer cooperatives as procuring milk and providing inputs and services, with management and technology made available to members. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The same study estimated roughly 17 percent higher milk production over five years in its study area, associated with marketing and animal services. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Main contributions

- IRMA was founded in 1979 to develop managers for cooperatives and other underserved rural organizations, showing how Kurien treated management talent as part of infrastructure. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Kurien served as founding chair of the National Dairy Development Board from 1965 to 1998, giving continuity to the national cooperative programme. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- IRMA credits Kurien with building supply-chain, production and marketing capabilities at Kaira; this is an institutional interpretation of a collaborative project. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Kurien identifies buffalo-milk powder as a pivotal technical breakthrough, because it let cooperatives preserve and market milk solids from a locally abundant source. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- NDDB reports 43,000 village cooperatives and 4.25 million producer members by 1985; these are programme records, not a count of all Indian dairy farmers. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- NDDB states that the National Milk Grid linked producers with urban consumers and reduced seasonal and regional price variations. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Leadership and relational characteristics

- GCMMF credits Tribhuvandas K. Patel as the cooperative’s founding chairman and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel as a supporter; the Kaira movement preceded Kurien’s arrival. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- In Kurien’s account, trained spearhead teams helped producers form institutions they owned and controlled, then withdrew support; that approach aimed to build local capacity rather than permanent outside management. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- IRMA emphasizes Kurien’s use of professional management in a farmer-owned enterprise; the combination made cooperative scale more viable than volunteer administration alone. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Kurien explicitly credits H. M. Dalaya, rather than himself, with making buffalo-milk powder technically possible and supplying Amul’s technical backbone. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contested material meeting the inclusion standard

- Claims about the White Revolution should credit farmers, Tribhuvandas Patel, and H. M. Dalaya, while independent evaluation cautions against attributing all milk-production growth or poverty reduction to Operation Flood alone. [S1] [S6] [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contemporary relevance

- In those study villages, improved marketing and competition were estimated to raise average producer milk prices by about 8 percent; the estimate is local, not a national average. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- NDDB calls Kurien an architect of the White Revolution; the label recognizes his leadership but should be read alongside farmer, cooperative, government and technical contributions. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- NDDB records growth in women members and women’s dairy societies during Phase III; membership growth alone does not show equal control over income or governance. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The evaluation links the dairy expansion to policy changes, demand growth and supply responses, rather than a single leader or institution; it reports declining real prices in its period of study. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
