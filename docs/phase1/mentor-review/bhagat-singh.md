- The hunger-strikers highlighted unequal treatment of Indian and European prisoners, connecting prison protest to colonial racial hierarchy. [S6] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_
- The prisoners demanded better food, clothing, reading material and newspapers, making prison conditions and intellectual access part of their political protest. [S6] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_

## Contemporary relevance

- The National Archives guide records Bhagat Singh’s execution on 23 March 1931, alongside Rajguru and Sukhdev in the Lahore Conspiracy Case context. [S7] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_
- Bhagat Singh’s distinctive-hatted portrait began as revolutionary propaganda and became an exceptionally recognizable nationalist image. [S8] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_
- Bhagat Singh’s execution helped create a powerful martyr memory, but scholarship warns against allowing martyrdom to eclipse his unfinished intellectual project. [S1] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_
- Bhagat Singh’s posthumous legacy is contested rather than fixed: nationalism, youth, sacrifice, socialism and dissent have all been emphasized by different later interpreters. [S1] _(approved claim; system_test_bulk)_
