## Profile introduction

- Britannica describes the Brahmo Samaj as a theistic movement within Hinduism founded in Calcutta in 1828 by Ram Mohun Roy that does not accept the authority of the Vedas or belief in avatars; the Order's biography agrees on the 1828 founding. [S1] [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Brahmo leader Pratap Chandra Mozoomdar, an admirer, wrote to Max Müller in 1895 that Ramakrishna knew no Sanskrit and perhaps not enough Bengali, attributing his wisdom to "genius and practical observation"; Müller found the judgement impartial, and the Order's biography likewise says he hardly knew how to write his name. [S3] [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Important qualities

- A scholarly reference entry summarises that he expanded his religious experience beyond Hinduism, first devoting three days to Allah and, years later, four to Christ, with visionary realisations he held to be the same as those of the Hindu divinities; these became the experiential basis for his claim that all religions can lead to the same realization. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Scholars read his gospel of the truth of all religions as based less on the belief that all lead to one formless Absolute than on his own experience of divine power at work in every manifest form, in line with his saying yato mat, tato path ("as many paths as points of view"). [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Scholars treat the Tantric training under the female adept Yogeshvari as formative: it helped turn his self-destructive longing into the joyful "play" (lila) of a child in the Mother's "mansion of mirth", and gave him a framework for integrating his religious experiences. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The disciple Saradananda gives the same episode in the Master's own reported words — despair at not seeing the Mother, the sword, a vision, and then unconsciousness with "a current of intense bliss"; it is first-generation disciple testimony drawn from Ramakrishna's reminiscences. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Main contributions

- The Order says Ramakrishna wrote no book and gave no public lectures, but spoke in simple language with parables and metaphors drawn from nature and daily life, which attracted Bengal's cultural elite. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Order's introduction to Saradananda's Lilaprasanga says it rests on first-hand observation and careful sifting of evidence, yet "may easily be ranked as one of the best specimens of hagiographic literature" — an acknowledgement that this disciple biography is devotional in character. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

[S3] [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Neevel and Hatcher describe Vivekananda’s Practical Vedanta as a creative contribution to the movement and report scholarly theories connecting it to Western morality and Western reconstructions of Vedanta. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Scholars explain his appeal in colonial Calcutta: his visitors had been schooled in reason, learning and social progress, but his disdain for book-learning and for the clerk's world, and the authenticity of his experience, made them re-examine the dignity of their spiritual heritage. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Leadership and relational characteristics

- Vivekananda said in a 1896 lecture that any word of truth or spirituality he had spoken anywhere he owed to his Master, adding "only the mistakes are mine" — his own testimony, not independent evidence of what Ramakrishna taught. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Scholars add that Narendranath, a Brahmo who believed in a formless God, first met Ramakrishna in 1881 (the Gospel chronology lists 1880), and was impressed by his renunciation but disturbed by his image-oriented worship of Kali and apparent lack of social concern. [S8] [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- After Ramakrishna's death Sarada became "Sarada Ma, the Holy Mother" (d. 1920), a status that scholars read as reflecting both her service to him and the power of the Divine Mother. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Belur Math says that after Ramakrishna's death Sarada Devi, brought to Kolkata by his disciples, began to accept spiritual seekers as her disciples, becoming in its words "the open portal to immortality for hundreds of people" and embracing all without distinction. [S9] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contested material meeting the inclusion standard

- Fifteen years later two Ramakrishna Order monastics, Swami Tyagananda and Pravrajika Vrajaprana, argued in Interpreting Ramakrishna (2010) that Kripal's Bengali-to-English translations were significantly flawed; Kripal replied that his mistakes were few and did not affect his main arguments. [S10] [S11] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Sil notes that Saradananda's story of Gadadhar's attempted suicide and his vision of Kali rests solely on Ramakrishna's own reminiscences and is not corroborated by other contemporary accounts; M. himself had "no clue" as to its veracity. [S12] [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Sil's notes flag passages where, he says, Nikhilananda's English Gospel omits sentences of the Bengali Kathamrita — for example one from a July 1884 conversation. [S12] [S10] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contemporary relevance

- A scholarly reference entry says Vivekananda instituted the Ramakrishna Mission on 1 May 1897, organising the monks in a new Ramakrishna Math; it notes that his plan to turn the disciples into monks dedicated to social service (seva) was one he claimed Ramakrishna had intended. [S8] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Hinduism Today, published by a Hindu monastic order, reports that on 2 July 1995 India's Supreme Court declared that neither Ramakrishna nor Vivekananda founded an independent, non-Hindu religion, ending the Mission's attempt to claim minority-religion privileges for its schools. [S13] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
