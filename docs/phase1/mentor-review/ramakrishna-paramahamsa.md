## Important qualities

- Visiting the scholar Vidyasagar on 5 August 1882, Ramakrishna is recorded saying that the Vedas, Puranas, Tantras and philosophies have been "defiled" by being spoken, while Brahman alone has not, since no one can say what it is; Vidyasagar is recorded replying that he had learnt something new. [S6] [S7] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Alongside his acceptance of all paths, M. records him telling a Brahmo devotee that the Eternal Religion of the rishis contains every form of worship, whereas "the modern cults" will last a few days and disappear — a reminder that his inclusiveness was framed within Hindu tradition. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- He distinguishes the jnani, who reaches Brahman by negating the world step by step, from the vijnani, who then finds that the steps are made of the same material as the roof — that Brahman has itself become the universe and living beings. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- In M.'s record of a conversation on 13 August 1882, Ramakrishna says God can be realized through all paths and that all religions are true, comparing the paths to stone stairs, wooden stairs, bamboo steps or a rope by which one reaches the same roof. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Main contributions

- Max Müller's 1898 book on Ramakrishna drew on a written account that he asked Vivekananda, one of Ramakrishna's "most eminent pupils", to supply; the Encyclopedia of Religion lists the book as an early account by a Westerner. [S5] [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Ramakrishna left no written work; his conversations from 1882 to 1886 were recorded in Bengali by Mahendranath Gupta ("M."), as the Kathamrita, which most English readers know through Nikhilananda's translation, The Gospel of Sri Ramakrishna. [S4] [S7] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Order says Ramakrishna wrote no book and gave no public lectures, but spoke in simple language with parables and metaphors drawn from nature and daily life, which attracted Bengal's cultural elite. [S8] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Order's introduction to Saradananda's Lilaprasanga says it rests on first-hand observation and careful sifting of evidence, yet "may easily be ranked as one of the best specimens of hagiographic literature" — an acknowledgement that this disciple biography is devotional in character. [S9] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Life and historical/social context

- The Brahmo leader Pratap Chandra Mozoomdar, an admirer, wrote to Max Müller in 1895 that Ramakrishna knew no Sanskrit and perhaps not enough Bengali, attributing his wisdom to "genius and practical observation"; Müller found the judgement impartial, and the Order's biography likewise says he hardly knew how to write his name. [S5] [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Ramakrishna Order's biography says Gadadhar, later Ramakrishna, was born on 18 February 1836 at Kamarpukur, a village in Hooghly district, Bengal, to a devout brahmin family. [S2] [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Challenges and limits

- In M.'s record of 6 December 1884, Ramakrishna says that selfless work (karma yoga) is a way to God but very difficult and unsuited to the present age, and that helping others is the work of God alone; a man's duty is to take refuge in God. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- In the Order's account Ramakrishna was grateful to the Divine Mother for sending him a disciple who doubted his realizations, often asking Narendra to test him as money-changers test coins, and laughing at his biting criticism of his samadhi. [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Leadership and relational characteristics

- In a boat talk with Keshab Chandra Sen on 27 October 1882, Ramakrishna is recorded saying that God alone is the Teacher, that his own attitude toward God is that of a child toward its mother, and that human gurus can be had by the million but few want to be disciples. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Vivekananda said in a 1896 lecture that any word of truth or spirituality he had spoken anywhere he owed to his Master, adding "only the mistakes are mine" — his own testimony, not independent evidence of what Ramakrishna taught. [S10] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- In the Order's account of their first meetings, Narendranath, a Brahmo college student of eighteen, sang for Ramakrishna, who took him aside and wept that he had come "very late"; Narendra, startled, thought "He must be stark mad". [S3] [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Scholars add that Narendranath, a Brahmo who believed in a formless God, first met Ramakrishna in 1881 (the Gospel chronology lists 1880), and was impressed by his renunciation but disturbed by his image-oriented worship of Kali and apparent lack of social concern. [S3] [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Contested material meeting the inclusion standard

- The Gospel's translator, Nikhilananda, adds a note that Ramakrishna's frequent phrase kamini-kanchan ("woman and gold") meant only "lust and greed" and that he used "woman" as a concrete term for the sex instinct when addressing male devotees; critics dispute such translation choices. [S6] [S11] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Whether the Mission's ethic of service continues Ramakrishna's own teaching is debated: scholars note the Kathamrita shows his outspoken impatience with those trying to improve the world, while Order sources read his compassion for the poor as service of God in man. [S4] [S6] [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Fifteen years later two Ramakrishna Order monastics, Swami Tyagananda and Pravrajika Vrajaprana, argued in Interpreting Ramakrishna (2010) that Kripal's Bengali-to-English translations were significantly flawed; Kripal replied that his mistakes were few and did not affect his main arguments. [S11] [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Jeffrey Kripal's Kali's Child (1995) applied psychoanalytic theory to Ramakrishna and argued that homoeroticism and sexual abuse lay at the secret heart of his life and work; the Encyclopedia of Religion adds that Kripal rejected reducing his spirituality to pathology. ## Contemporary relevance

- In M.'s record of 6 December 1884, Ramakrishna says that selfless work (karma yoga) is a way to God but very difficult and unsuited to the present age, and that helping others is the work of God alone; a man's duty is to take refuge in God. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- A scholarly reference entry says Vivekananda instituted the Ramakrishna Mission on 1 May 1897, organising the monks in a new Ramakrishna Math; it notes that his plan to turn the disciples into monks dedicated to social service (seva) was one he claimed Ramakrishna had intended. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- After Ramakrishna's death in August 1886 the young disciples gathered at a house at Baranagore rented with help from the householder Surendra, which gradually became the first monastery of the Order. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Hinduism Today, published by a Hindu monastic order, reports that on 2 July 1995 India's Supreme Court declared that neither Ramakrishna nor Vivekananda founded an independent, non-Hindu religion, ending the Mission's attempt to claim minority-religion privileges for its schools. [S12] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
