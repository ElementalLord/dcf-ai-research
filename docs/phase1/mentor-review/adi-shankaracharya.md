## Important qualities

- In Shankara’s account, ritual and ethical action can purify the mind and prepare it for knowledge, but cannot themselves produce moksha as an action-result. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Shankara reads the Upanishads as teaching that Atman, the deepest self, and Brahman, ultimate reality, are one and the same. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Shankara rejects the view that liberation comes from combining Vedic action with knowledge; knowledge of Brahman alone liberates, while disciplined action prepares the mind. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Shankara treats avidya, ignorance, as beginningless; asking for its first origin already assumes the ignorant standpoint it is meant to diagnose. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Main contributions

- A Sanskrit Documents repository preserves a text labeled Bhagavad Gita Shankara Bhashya; the repository’s attribution documents received textual tradition, while scholarly authorship assessment remains separate. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Upanishads, Brahma Sutra and Bhagavad Gita form Vedanta’s core canonical triad; Shankara’s commentaries on them made interpretation of this triad central to Advaita. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Shankara treats the Upanishads not only as authorities but as texts that model methods of interpretation, using literary criteria to establish a coherent teaching. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

- Mandana Misra represents an important near-contemporary Advaita-related interlocutor: he gave greater weight than Shankara to combining knowledge and action on the path to liberation. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Post-Shankara Advaita developed major Bhamati and Vivarana sub-schools, especially through differing accounts of avidya; later Advaita should not be automatically projected back onto Shankara. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
