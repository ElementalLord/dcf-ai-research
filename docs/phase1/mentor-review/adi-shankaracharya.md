## Important qualities

- Shankara reads the Upanishads as teaching that Atman, the deepest self, and Brahman, ultimate reality, are one and the same. [S3] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Shankara rejects the view that liberation comes from combining Vedic action with knowledge; knowledge of Brahman alone liberates, while disciplined action prepares the mind. [S2] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Shankara’s teaching tradition joins scriptural listening, reflection and contemplation as learning practices directed toward liberating knowledge. [S1] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- His significance lies in systematic nondual Upanishadic interpretation and a knowledge-centered path to liberation; received commentarial attributions require separate assessment. [S3] [S2] [S4] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
