## Important qualities

- Rabindra-Bhavana’s holdings document Tagore’s work in literature, correspondence, painting, music and institutional life. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Surviving voice recordings and musical materials support study of Rabindra Sangeet as a performed tradition as well as a body of written compositions. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Amartya Sen describes Tagore as a towering presence in Bengali culture whose writings and songs remain active in everyday cultural life in India and Bangladesh. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Gitanjali explores devotion, mortality, work, nature and freedom through lyric encounters between the finite person and a larger spiritual reality. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Main contributions

- Tagore founded Visva-Bharati in 1921, building on his earlier Santiniketan school. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore opened the Brahmacharyasrama school at Santiniketan in December 1901 with only five students. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore received the 1913 Nobel Prize in Literature after the English Gitanjali had attracted intense attention in Britain and beyond. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore worked across poetry, short stories, novels, drama, essays and song, so describing him only as the author of Gitanjali obscures the range of his career. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Bichitra archive preserves manuscript and printed witnesses that allow scholars to trace Tagore’s revisions instead of treating every circulated wording as stable. [S8] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Visva-Bharati’s history says Tagore’s Santiniketan school held classes outdoors under trees as part of an education linking people and nature. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Life and historical/social context

- British India Office correspondence records Tagore’s renunciation of the knighthood in 1919 after the Punjab violence and Jallianwala Bagh massacre. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Rabindranath Tagore was born in Calcutta on 7 May 1861 into a prominent Bengali family engaged in literary, religious and social reform. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore began writing poetry early, but his intellectual formation also drew on music, family networks, travel and sustained engagement with Bengali and European traditions. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore’s Nationalism lectures emerged during the First World War, when imperial rivalry and mass national organization gave his warnings immediate historical force. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Challenges and limits

- Tagore’s early Western reception often narrowed him into a spiritual sage, an image that left his fiction, social criticism, irony and institutional work less visible. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Leadership and relational characteristics

- Tagore’s correspondence is essential evidence for his relationships with contemporaries because later anecdotes can oversimplify disagreement, friendship and influence. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- W. B. Yeats’s introduction helped frame the English Gitanjali for British readers and was part of the network that carried Tagore into international literary circulation. [S6] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore respected Gandhi’s political achievement and helped popularize the honorific Mahatma, even while the two disagreed publicly. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Tagore–Gandhi relationship combined friendship and principled disagreement over nationalism, education, science and protest, illustrating competing visions of Indian freedom. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contemporary relevance

- Gitanjali had already been repeatedly reprinted in London before the Nobel announcement, showing that its international reception preceded the prize. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore became the first Asian Nobel laureate, a landmark in the global recognition of modern Indian literature. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Visva-Bharati became a central university and institution of national importance in 1951, extending Tagore’s educational experiment into a durable public institution. [S7] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Tagore warned Japan and other Asian societies against imitating the aggressive nationalism through which Western powers had dominated others. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
