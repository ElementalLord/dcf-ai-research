## Profile introduction

- Satyajit Ray was born in Calcutta on 2 May 1921 into a family associated with literature, art, music, and printing. [S1] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Ray received the Academy honor shortly before his death in April 1992, at the end of a film career spanning more than three decades. [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Important qualities

- Ravi Shankar composed the music for Pather Panchali; crediting the early score to Ray would confuse the director’s role with that of a major collaborator. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Ray progressively took responsibility for composing his own film scores and songs, adding music to his integrated control of later productions. [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Ray described the Goopy-Bagha music as drawing on Indian folk and classical forms alongside Western orchestration, not as belonging to one pure tradition. [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Main contributions

- Aparajito and Apur Sansar followed Pather Panchali and completed the Apu Trilogy. [S1] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Charulata adapts a work by Tagore. [S5] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Ray’s documented identity spans directing, screenwriting, design, illustration, editing, writing, and composition; the Apu Trilogy was one major part of this broader multi-art career. [S6] [S2] [S7] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- An Indian government film catalogue documented Ray’s established professional identity across directing, editing, screenwriting and music direction. [S8] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- At Signet Press, Ray designed covers and illustrated books, work that trained him to condense a narrative’s tone into visual form. [S7] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Film prints, scripts, designs and photographs allow scholars to reconstruct Ray’s working process beyond retrospective legend. [S9] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Leadership and relational characteristics

- Pather Panchali joined neorealist encouragement, close collaboration, and public financial support; accounts of its authorship should preserve Ray’s direction and those collective conditions. [S6] [S3] _(approved claim; human_manual)_
- Art director Bansi Chandragupta helped construct the material environments through which Ray’s realism became visually convincing. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Cinematographer Subrata Mitra was a central collaborator in creating Pather Panchali’s visual world and later worked with Ray on several major films. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The debut relied on a largely inexperienced team whose learning during production became part of the film’s distinctive achievement. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Contemporary relevance

- Pather Panchali’s festival circulation brought wide international attention to Ray and to a form of Bengali cinema outside dominant commercial conventions. [S6] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Academy honored Ray for a lifetime body of work rather than awarding a competitive Oscar to Pather Panchali or another individual film. [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Academy’s citation explicitly recognized Ray’s international influence on filmmakers and audiences, evidence of institutional reception rather than a neutral measure of all influence. [S2] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Calling Ray a complete filmmaker is meaningful when it refers to his documented work across scripting, direction, design, music and editing, not when it erases collaborators. [S7] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
