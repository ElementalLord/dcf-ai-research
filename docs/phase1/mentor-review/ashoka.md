## Contemporary relevance

- Ashoka’s later image as an ideal dharmaraja is a history of reception; it cannot be read back without qualification as a transparent portrait of third-century BCE rule. [S7] [S8] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The modern rediscovery of Ashoka combines epigraphy, colonial-era scholarship, archaeology and later political memory rather than a simple recovery of an unchanged ancient reputation. [S7] [S8] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- India adopted the Ashoka Chakra on its national flag in 1947, a later national use of an Ashokan symbol rather than evidence of his own constitutional ideas. [S9] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Modern India has used Ashoka as a unifying public figure; this national symbolism is historically significant while remaining distinct from the Mauryan emperor’s own inscriptions. [S9] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
