## Main contributions

- At the 1963 Edinburgh International Festival she gave two Carnatic recitals, placing a major Indian vocal tradition before an international festival audience. [S1] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Her Edinburgh visit was followed by BBC recordings and a London recital, extending the reach of her voice beyond the festival hall. [S1] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- In 1968 she became the first woman to preside over the Music Academy’s annual conference and receive its Sangita Kalanidhi title, crossing an institutional barrier in a central Carnatic forum. [S1] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Secretary-General U Thant said he invited her and accompanying artists for an extra concert connected to International Co-operation Year; the invitation was postponed before the 1966 recital. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Subbulakshmi performed at the United Nations in October 1966. A UN press statement heads the concert 21 October, while a later UN archival caption and other accounts give 23 October, so the exact day remains qualified. [S3] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The 1963 European tour concluded with a Cairo performance and an encounter with Egyptian singer Umm Kulthum, placing her within a wider transnational world of celebrated vocalists. [S1] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_

## Challenges and limits

- Desiraju notes that 1930s and 1940s Madras sabhas could segregate women listeners and price their tickets differently, evidence that concert access was socially structured even as women performers gained prominence. [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- Desiraju remembers Subbulakshmi as a dominant presence amid major mid-century Carnatic contemporaries such as G. N. Balasubramaniam, Madurai Mani Iyer, Semmangudi Srinivasa Iyer, Ariyakudi Ramanuja Iyengar, D. K. Pattammal and M. L. Vasanthakumari; the comparison shows she rose in a crowded, highly skilled concert world. [S4] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
- The Music Academy’s account of its programmes shows that Carnatic concerts in her period were being formalized around ragas, compositions, languages, accompanists and printed programmes; her career unfolded within this changing public concert culture. ## Leadership and relational characteristics

- She married T. Sadasivam in 1940; contemporary accounts identify his role in guiding and organizing her later public career. [S1] _(draft; awaiting human decision; ai_candidate)_
