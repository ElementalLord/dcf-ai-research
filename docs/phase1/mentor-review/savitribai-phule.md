## Important qualities

- Savitribai published Kavyaphule in 1854; her writing addressed social and educational questions alongside nature and instruction, making her a thinker and author as well as a schoolteacher. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Savitribai served as headmistress in the Phules’ school network; her contribution included educational responsibility and organization, beyond being Jyotirao’s spouse. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Savitribai taught at the Phules’ girls’ school in Bhidewada, Pune, opened in 1848, turning the idea of female education into an operating institution. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The university chronology records a night school in 1855, placing the Phules’ educational effort beyond girls’ schooling and within wider provision for adult learners. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Main contributions

- After Jyotirao’s death in 1890, Savitribai continued leading Satyashodhak activity; her public contribution extended beyond work performed while her husband was alive. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- In 1868 Jyotirao opened the household well to people denied access to public water by untouchability, an action of the Phule household against everyday caste segregation. [S3] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Jyotirao established the Satyashodhak Samaj in September 1873 to organize a sustained challenge to caste and religious hierarchy; this supplied the collective setting for Savitribai’s later work. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Savitribai faced opposition for teaching girls and disadvantaged caste groups; this resistance reveals that her schools challenged who was entitled to knowledge and a public role. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Phules organized education through institutions for girls and for Mahar and Mang communities, giving excluded learners continuing access rather than relying on isolated charitable lessons. [S4] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- The Satyashodhak Samaj promoted low-cost marriages without priestly mediation or dowry, contesting social authority through practical changes in family institutions. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_

## Contemporary relevance

- Azim Premji University retells Savitribai’s life through Patua-style illustrations, evidence of her later role as an educational exemplar; the dramatized scenes and dialogue are modern interpretation. [S2] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- During Pune’s plague outbreak in 1897, Savitribai worked with her physician son Yeshwant to provide treatment and bring patients to a hospital, according to Narake. [S1] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- Her letter of 20 April 1877 describes Satyashodhak committees and relief squads distributing essential supplies during famine, evidence of organized social service as part of the reform movement. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
- In the same 1877 letter, Savitribai reports negotiating with the Collector for detained volunteers and securing food aid, showing relief work involved dealing with colonial administrative power. [S5] _(approved claim; corpus_evidence_review)_
